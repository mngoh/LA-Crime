Assault victims in Los Angeles

LAPD reports, January 2020 to December 2023 · 119,249 assaults · Martin Ngoh

Code & notebooks martinngoh.com
The question

Black women are a far larger share of assault victims than women of any other group. Is that a higher rate of victimization, or just where assaults happen?

A higher rate. Per 100,000 residents a year, Black women are assaulted 1,859 times: 3 times the rate of Hispanic women, 5.4 times White women, 17.2 times Asian women, and more often than Hispanic or White men.

Adjusted for age, year, where the assault happened, neighborhood poverty, income, unemployment, housing and homelessness, the gap is still about 2 times Hispanic or White women and 10 times Asian women. The model is below.

Findings

The rate gap is real

Black women's victimization rate is 1,859 per 100,000 a year. Hispanic women: 623. White women: 342. Asian women: 108. Population share does not explain it.

Closest to parity with men

Black women are assaulted at 79% of the rate of Black men. For Hispanic, White and Asian women the figure is 62%, 61% and 56%.

Simple assault is where women outnumber men

56.7% of Black simple-assault victims are women, the only group above 50%. In aggravated assault the share is 34.7%, against 24.5% to 26.8% for other groups.

South LA carries the counts, Central the rate

77th Street (2,449) and Southeast (1,935) have the most Black female victims. Central has the highest rate (7,242) on a small resident population.

Mostly strong-arm

58% of all assaults involved hands, fists or feet rather than a weapon. Handguns appear in 7,111.

What this does not show

Why. These are reported assaults against residential population. The caveats below matter as much as the numbers.

Testing explanations

Six cuts of the same data, women only (12,393 Black women, 30,892 other women). Each asks whether a plain explanation accounts for the gap.

Age

Does the gap survive comparing women of the same age? Yes. Standardized to one age mix, Black women's rate is 1,814 against 621, 336 and 101. The curve peaks at ages 25-29.

Location

Is it where assaults happen? Partly. At other women's rate in each division, Black women would be at 584 per 100,000 instead of 1,861. Inside every rated division their rate is still 2.5 to 5.6 times other women's.

Type of assault

Simple or aggravated? Both, and aggravated more: 2.5 times Hispanic women for simple assault, 4.2 times for aggravated.

Time

Does it persist? Every year. Black women: 1,818, 1,811, 1,879 and 1,929 per 100,000.

Premises

Different places? Barely. Street 22.0% vs 19.0%, at home 38.3% vs 34.8%. Share of each group's assaults, top eight premises. Intimate partner assaults are coded separately by LAPD and covered in their own section below.

Weapons

Different circumstances? Yes. A firearm in 12.2% of assaults on Black women against 6.4% for other women; strong-arm 60.9% vs 71.4%.

Reporting: not testable here

LAPD data holds only what was reported. The National Crime Victimization Survey measures unreported crime nationally, not for Los Angeles. If Black women report assaults more or less often than other women, every rate above moves, and this data cannot say which way.

What remains

After age, location and year, a gap of roughly two and a half to three stands, widest in aggravated and armed assaults. The model below puts numbers on it. The data shows the gap; it does not explain it.

The model

One question, answered one adjustment at a time: how much of the gap survives? Poisson rate models at the census-tract level, women only, 33,120 tract by group by age by year cells, 81.7% of located Black women victims and 98.2% of other women (the rest were assaulted in tracts with no resident women of their group and age, so they have no denominator). Each bar is Black women's rate as a multiple of other women's after the controls named.

Surviving rate ratio

Crude 3.36x. After age, year, where it happened and tract poverty, income, unemployment, renters and density: 2.44x (95% CI 2.35 to 2.54). Controls account for about 39% of the excess; the rest stands.

Fully adjusted, by assault type

The same controls, run separately. Simple assault 2.13x, aggravated assault 3.28x. The serious end of the distribution is where the gap resists explanation.

Each comparison group on its own

Pooling hides a spread. Fully adjusted, Black women's rate is 2.07 times Hispanic women's, 2.3 times White women's and 10.42 times Asian women's. Crude gaps in grey outline.

Does the homelessness measure matter?

LAHSA publishes counts, not person estimates, by tract. Three definitions give the same answer: 2.44x, 2.45x, 2.44x. The covariate is not driving the result.

What the model says

Where assaults happen is the biggest single factor, removing about 31% of the excess. Tract poverty, income, unemployment, renters and density together remove another 4% once location is in. Age removes 3%; year, nothing. A 2.44-fold gap remains that none of these measured factors explain.

What it cannot say

Residents are the denominator, so exposure away from home is unmeasured, and 18% of Black women victims were assaulted in tracts with no resident women like them. Homelessness enters only as a tract-level count from the 2024 LAHSA count, a proxy for exposure, not a measure of who the victims were. Reporting behavior is invisible to police data.

A second records system, and intimate partner assault

Two checks on data the analysis above does not use. LAPD moved to the FBI's NIBRS standard in March 2024, so June 2024 to August 2026 is a separate test on new records. And LAPD's old system coded intimate partner assaults separately (codes 626 and 236); they are not in the rates or model above. Here both are counted the same way in both systems, women only, per 100,000 residents a year.

The gap replicates

Non-partner assault. Black women's rate is 2.83 times Hispanic women's and 5.51 times White women's in NIBRS, against 2.98 and 5.44 before. The Asian comparison moved most, from 17.21 to 10.8.

Intimate partner assault

Nearly half of all assaults on women in every group. Black women's partner assault rate is 1,685, 2.73 times Hispanic and 6.02 times White women's. 2020 to 2023.

Same gap, new system

Different years, a different records system and different offense coding, and Black women's rate is still about 3 times Hispanic women's and 5.5 times White women's.

Not concentrated at home

Intimate partner assault is 47.5% of assaults on Black women and 49.7% for Hispanic women. The gap is the same size with partners as without. Counting both, Black women are assaulted at 3,544 per 100,000 a year, about 1 in 28.

Context: this is the direction national evidence points. The National Crime Victimization Survey, which counts crimes whether or not they were reported, found the rate of violence against Black women almost 50% higher than against White women in 2005, by strangers, acquaintances and partners alike (Heimer and colleagues, BJS; Harrell, BJS 2007). Los Angeles's police-recorded gap is larger than that national survey figure, which is itself worth explaining.

Caveats

This shows what, not why

The data says Black women are assaulted at a higher rate. It does not say why. Nothing here measures causes, offenders or circumstances.

Reported crimes only

Every number is a report that reached LAPD. Willingness to report, and police recording practice, differ by group, by area and over time. A higher rate can partly reflect more reporting.

Exposure is not population

Rates divide by where people live, not where they spend time. Someone who works, commutes or socializes in a high-assault area carries that exposure home to a different denominator.

Residential denominators

Divisions with many visitors, workers or unhoused residents, Central above all, show inflated rates because victims there often do not live there.

Who is counted

The main rates and model cover simple and aggravated assault (LAPD codes 624 and 230); intimate partner assaults are compared separately. 10,238 victims with unknown race or sex are left out of the rates. LAPD descent codes are officer-recorded and were collapsed to four groups; everyone else is excluded.

Period and population mismatch

Victims cover January 2020 to December 2023, a window that includes the pandemic. Population is the ACS 2020 to 2024 five-year average, with sampling error at the tract level.

Method

LAPD victim descent codes mapped to four groups (Asian combines the Asian descent codes). Population by census tract from the ACS 2020 to 2024 five-year release via Census Reporter; 1,110 tracts inside the city were assigned to LAPD divisions by tract centroid using the city's division boundaries (11 fell outside). Rates divide victims by residents and by 4.0 years. Cells with fewer than 2,000 residents are not rated.

Sources: LAPD Open Data (Crime Data from 2020 to 2024; NIBRS Victims), US Census Bureau ACS, LA GeoHub, LAHSA. NIBRS assaults are offense codes 13A and 13B, classed as intimate partner by their offense label; officer and child victims are excluded in both systems. NIBRS labels do not map one-to-one to the old codes, so compare ratios between groups rather than levels between systems. Reproduce with the scripts in the repository.
