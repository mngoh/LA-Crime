Assault victims in Los Angeles

LAPD reports, January 2020 to June 2023 · 100,946 assaults · Martin Ngoh

Code & notebooks martinngoh.com
The question

Black women are a far larger share of assault victims than women of any other group. Is that a higher rate of victimization, or just where assaults happen?

A higher rate. Per 100,000 residents a year, Black women are assaulted 1,803 times: 3 times the rate of Hispanic women, 5.4 times White women, 17 times Asian women, and more often than Hispanic or White men.

Adjusted for age, year, where the assault happened, neighborhood poverty, income, unemployment, housing and homelessness, the gap is still about 2 times Hispanic or White women and 10 times Asian women. The model is below.

Findings

The rate gap is real

Black women's victimization rate is 1,803 per 100,000 a year. Hispanic women: 598. White women: 335. Asian women: 106. Population share does not explain it.

Closest to parity with men

Black women are assaulted at 79% of the rate of Black men. For Hispanic, White and Asian women the figure is 62%, 61% and 56%.

Simple assault is where women outnumber men

56.5% of Black simple-assault victims are women, the only group above 50%. In aggravated assault the share is 34.4%, against 24.5% to 27.1% for other groups.

South LA carries the counts, Central the rate

77th Street (2,069) and Southeast (1,658) have the most Black female victims. Central has the highest rate (7,124) on a small resident population.

Mostly strong-arm

58% of all assaults involved hands, fists or feet rather than a weapon. Handguns appear in 6,029.

What this does not show

Why. These are reported assaults against residential population. The caveats below matter as much as the numbers.

Testing explanations

Six cuts of the same data, women only (10,518 Black women, 26,088 other women). Each asks whether a plain explanation accounts for the gap.

Age

Does the gap survive comparing women of the same age? Yes. Standardized to one age mix, Black women's rate is 1,758 against 596, 328 and 99. The curve peaks at ages 20-24.

Location

Is it where assaults happen? Partly. At other women's rate in each division, Black women would be at 560 per 100,000 instead of 1,806. Inside every rated division their rate is still 2.5 to 5.6 times other women's.

Type of assault

Simple or aggravated? Both, and aggravated more: 2.6 times Hispanic women for simple assault, 4.2 times for aggravated.

Time

Does it persist? Every year. Black women: 1,818, 1,809, 1,876 and 1,618 annualized for the first half of 2023.

Premises

Different places? Barely. Street 22.1% vs 18.8%, at home 38.2% vs 35.1%. Share of each group's assaults, top eight premises. A slightly larger share at home and on the street is consistent with domestic and neighborhood violence, but LAPD's file has no victim-offender relationship field, so this data cannot confirm it.

Weapons

Different circumstances? Yes. A firearm in 12.4% of assaults on Black women against 6.4% for other women; strong-arm 60.6% vs 71.2%.

Reporting: not testable here

LAPD data holds only what was reported. The National Crime Victimization Survey measures unreported crime nationally, not for Los Angeles. If Black women report assaults more or less often than other women, every rate above moves, and this data cannot say which way.

What remains

After age, location and year, a gap of roughly two and a half to three stands, widest in aggravated and armed assaults. The model below puts numbers on it. The data shows the gap; it does not explain it.

The model

One question, answered one adjustment at a time: how much of the gap survives? Poisson rate models at the census-tract level, women only, 33,120 tract by group by age by year cells, 81.6% of located Black women victims and 98.2% of other women (the rest were assaulted in tracts with no resident women of their group and age, so they have no denominator). Each bar is Black women's rate as a multiple of other women's after the controls named.

Surviving rate ratio

Crude 3.37x. After age, year, where it happened and tract poverty, income, unemployment, renters and density: 2.46x (95% CI 2.36 to 2.56). Controls account for about 38% of the excess; the rest stands.

Fully adjusted, by assault type

The same controls, run separately. Simple assault 2.14x, aggravated assault 3.29x. The serious end of the distribution is where the gap resists explanation.

Each comparison group on its own

Pooling hides a spread. Fully adjusted, Black women's rate is 2.09 times Hispanic women's, 2.26 times White women's and 10.31 times Asian women's. Crude gaps in grey outline.

Does the homelessness measure matter?

LAHSA publishes counts, not person estimates, by tract. Three definitions give the same answer: 2.46x, 2.47x, 2.45x. The covariate is not driving the result.

What the model says

Where assaults happen is the biggest single factor, removing about 31% of the excess. Tract poverty, income, unemployment, renters and density together remove another 4% once location is in. Age removes 3%; year, nothing. A 2.46-fold gap remains that none of these measured factors explain.

What it cannot say

Residents are the denominator, so exposure away from home is unmeasured, and 18% of Black women victims were assaulted in tracts with no resident women like them. Homelessness enters only as a tract-level count from the 2024 LAHSA count, a proxy for exposure, not a measure of who the victims were. Reporting behavior is invisible to police data.

Context: this is the direction national evidence points. The National Crime Victimization Survey, which counts crimes whether or not they were reported, found the rate of violence against Black women almost 50% higher than against White women in 2005, by strangers, acquaintances and partners alike (Heimer and colleagues, BJS; Harrell, BJS 2007). Los Angeles's police-recorded gap is larger than that national survey figure, which is itself worth explaining.

Caveats

This shows what, not why

The data says Black women are assaulted at a higher rate. It does not say why. Nothing here measures causes, offenders or circumstances.

Reported crimes only

Every number is a report that reached LAPD. Willingness to report, and police recording practice, differ by group, by area and over time. A higher rate can partly reflect more reporting.

Exposure is not population

Rates divide by where people live, not where they spend time. Someone who works, commutes or socializes in a high-assault area carries that exposure home to a different denominator.

Residential denominators

Divisions with many visitors, workers or unhoused residents, Central above all, show inflated rates because victims there often do not live there.

Who is counted

8,621 victims with unknown race or sex are left out of the rates. LAPD descent codes are officer-recorded and were collapsed to four groups; everyone else is excluded.

Period and population mismatch

Victims cover January 2020 to June 2023, a window that includes the pandemic. Population is the ACS 2020 to 2024 five-year average, with sampling error at the tract level.

Method

LAPD victim descent codes mapped to four groups (Asian combines the Asian descent codes). Population by census tract from the ACS 2020 to 2024 five-year release via Census Reporter; 1,110 tracts inside the city were assigned to LAPD divisions by tract centroid using the city's division boundaries (11 fell outside). Rates divide victims by residents and by 3.5 years. Cells with fewer than 2,000 residents are not rated.

Sources: LAPD Open Data (Crime Data from 2020 to Present), US Census Bureau ACS, LA GeoHub. Reproduce with scripts/compute_rates.py and scripts/build_page.py.
